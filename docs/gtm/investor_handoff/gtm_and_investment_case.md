# UAH and NeuralWorkbench GTM and Investment Case

**Prepared:** 2026-10-02

**Scope:** commercialization hypotheses, customer sequence, packaging,
milestones, metrics, use of capital, and diligence disclosures

**Important:** market size, pricing, demand, and sales-cycle assumptions have
not yet been validated in this repository

## 1. Commercial framing

UAH is positioned as infrastructure for teams that want to use language models
inside an existing operational stack without transferring authority to the
model or rebuilding the same harness controls for every role.

The proposed commercial outcome is a reusable control and evidence layer that
helps a customer:

- constrain each agent to a task-relative capability surface;
- retain native ownership of execution, safety, and evidence;
- change models or providers without changing role semantics;
- inspect the chain from raw output to admitted operation and native result;
- replay failures without invoking the model;
- compare models, prompts, policies, and candidate structures under frozen
  acceptance criteria;
- use NeuralWorkbench to retrieve and compare prior experience without granting
  online self-modification authority.

The commercial claim is not established until at least two materially different
domains use the unchanged kernel and onboarding effort is measured.

## 2. Beachhead hypothesis

The best initial design partner is expected to have four characteristics:

1. an existing multi-process or multi-owner system;
2. a concrete desire to add or replace an LLM role;
3. costly or visible failures that require evidence and replay;
4. enough task repetition to justify standardized qualification and memory.

Likely early segments include robotics and embodied-agent teams, local-first
software-agent deployments, research automation platforms, and operational AI
teams integrating several model providers. Regulated use may become relevant,
but the current project has no compliance certification and should not lead
with regulated-production claims.

### Reference sequence

| Sequence | Reference environment | Commercial purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | NAO planner | Demonstrate preserved native ownership, multi-actor traces, failure attribution, and no duplicate activation |
| 2 | Watson/software engineering | Demonstrate named agents, local-model portability, provider swapping, workspace evidence, and repeatable evaluation |
| 3 | iTrader or bounded market simulator | Demonstrate proposer/solver/verifier separation, simulator evidence, risk ownership, and held-out evaluation |
| 4 | Gamma or another research workflow | Demonstrate domain onboarding and cross-agent composition outside embodied control |

NAO is an engineering qualification environment, not necessarily the largest
commercial market. Watson is a practical second proof because the same Linux
workstation can exercise software-engineering tasks and local inference without
robot hardware.

## 3. Buyer and user hypotheses

| Role | Problem | Relevant product surface | Purchase evidence to obtain |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AI platform lead | Each team builds a different agent runtime and trace format | UAH kernel, model port, identity, conformance suite | Reduced integration duplication and faster role qualification |
| Robotics or automation lead | Model behavior must coexist with deterministic owners and lifecycle gates | Domain adapter, two-stage admission, evidence ledger | Parity, lower failure-diagnosis time, no ownership regression |
| Agent product lead | Model/provider changes make behavior and cost difficult to compare | Stable handles, frozen cases, Observatory comparisons | Faster model replacement with maintained acceptance |
| Reliability or evaluation lead | Chat logs do not show what was admitted or what the environment proved | Observatory, replay, VerifiedTraceDigest | Higher evidence completeness and reproducible incident review |
| Research lead | Prior trajectories are difficult to reuse safely | NeuralWorkbench retrieval and candidate evaluation | Held-out uplift without unsafe-action or evidence regression |

Economic buyer, procurement path, deployment preference, and willingness to pay
must be tested through interviews and paid design partnerships.

## 4. Product packaging hypothesis

### 4.1 UAH Core

Portable task compilation, semantic admission, identity, lifecycle, provider
ports, domain adapters, evidence normalization, acceptance, and replay.

Potential packaging:

- source-available or commercial enterprise runtime;
- self-hosted deployment for local and private models;
- conformance suite and signed release artifacts;
- supported adapters for selected runtimes.

Licensing has not been decided. The current repository license and every reused
dependency or companion revision require legal review before commercial
packaging.

### 4.2 Domain onboarding and qualification

A services-led engagement that produces:

- an approved DomainContractPack;
- environment profile and agent roster;
- bindings and normalization adapters;
- frozen success and failure fixtures;
- evidence and acceptance rules;
- deployment preflight and rollback plan;
- parity and qualification report.

This is the likely initial revenue motion. The business becomes scalable only
if the second and third onboarding efforts show declining custom engineering.

### 4.3 Observatory

Read-only operational and evaluation views over the common ledger. Potential
buyers may value incident reconstruction, model comparison, policy review,
evaluation, and evidence export even before NeuralWorkbench adaptation is
enabled.

### 4.4 NeuralWorkbench

An H3+ module for retrieval, candidate construction, failure-aware memory,
scoring, and shadow evaluation. Commercial activation should follow measured
uplift and remain optional. H4 crystallization and reviewed promotion may
support maintained interaction-skill libraries, but automatic self-publishing
is outside the product boundary.

### 4.5 Support and conformance

Potential recurring services include release qualification, domain-pack
version review, provider/model conformance, incident replay, security updates,
and migration support.

## 5. Founder-led GTM sequence

### Stage A: technical design partners

Select one or two partners with an existing system and a bounded high-value
agent role. Sell a qualification outcome rather than broad autonomy.

Deliverables:

- current-system seam and ownership audit;
- one frozen task and failure suite;
- one DomainContractPack and adapter;
- one explicit shadow or authority mode;
- one evidence and parity report;
- measured onboarding time and unresolved gaps.

Exit condition: a partner agrees that the evidence artifact is useful enough to
continue, pay, or provide a reference under defined terms.

### Stage B: reference deployment

Complete H2 NAO parity and one Watson/software-engineering deployment. Publish a
technical case study that reports failures and limitations alongside successes.

Exit condition: the unchanged kernel qualifies two frames, with measured reuse
and no domain-specific imports in core.

### Stage C: repeatable onboarding

Turn repeated work into an onboarding workflow, templates, validators, and
conformance tests. Keep generated domain content quarantined until owner review.

Exit condition: a third environment reaches shadow qualification with less
custom kernel work than the second.

### Stage D: adaptive module

Offer NeuralWorkbench to customers with repeated tasks and sufficient trace
volume. Begin with inspect-only retrieval and shadow candidate comparison.

Exit condition: held-out task results improve against a no-memory baseline
without worsening unsafe-action rate, evidence completeness, or rollback.

### Stage E: platform motion

Package supported runtimes, domain packs, Observatory views, conformance, and
Workbench policies into a repeatable enterprise deployment.

Exit condition: recurring product revenue and partner-led onboarding contribute
materially relative to bespoke engineering services.

## 6. Sales narrative by audience

### Engineering leadership

"Keep your orchestrator, lifecycle, safety, and native APIs. Add a portable
model control layer that compiles task scope and records what was proposed,
admitted, executed, and proven."

### Reliability and governance

"Replace session-only logs with replayable evidence that distinguishes model
claims from environment-owner proof and terminal acceptance."

### Model and infrastructure teams

"Treat the model as a leased compute resource beneath a stable agent identity,
then compare local and remote providers under the same role and task suite."

### Research teams

"Use completed traces, failures, and counterexamples to search interaction
structures in shadow mode before proposing a reviewed replacement."

These are positioning statements for discovery, not verified customer quotes.

## 7. Defensibility hypothesis

Potential defensibility comes from accumulated, interoperable operational
assets rather than a single model:

- the AB frame and object grammar;
- reviewed DomainContractPacks and adapters;
- frozen task and failure suites;
- multi-actor identity and evidence lineage;
- cross-provider and cross-runtime conformance data;
- supporting and counterexample trace corpora;
- Workbench retrieval, candidate, and crystallization evaluation;
- onboarding workflow and owner-review discipline.

These assets may produce switching costs and data advantage if they remain
portable and evidence-rich. They do not yet constitute a demonstrated moat.
Patentability, trade-secret policy, open-source strategy, and freedom to operate
require separate legal analysis.

## 8. Milestone-gated investment plan

Capital should be released against evidence-producing milestones rather than
feature count.

| Gate | Deliverable | Value created | Go or no-go evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| G1: executable semantic center | Operation edges, typed evidence/rejection grammar, terminal counterexample, and initial runtime controls | Extends accepted/deficit replay into model-free counterexample and control replay | Success and failure replay identically without the model |
| G2: H1 agent runtime | Extend initial agent/handle/standby registries with durable lifecycle, PromptCompiler, provider port, fixed lease, and startup preflight | Enables local and remote model trials under stable roles | Frozen suite runs through recorded and live provider modes |
| G3: H2 NAO parity | Domain pack, planner adapter, report-result delegation, `legacy | shadow | uah` report | Demonstrates integration with a real multi-owner system | Reviewed parity and failure attribution, no duplicate action or speech |
| G4: second-domain proof | Watson/software-engineering adapter using unchanged core | Tests portability and onboarding leverage | Measured reuse and same-model comparison |
| G5: H3 Workbench shadow | Verified trace digest, retrieval, candidate provenance, counterexample-aware memory | Tests adaptive advantage without authority risk | Held-out uplift or recovery improvement with safety/evidence parity |
| G6: repeatable commercialization | Paid partner, deployment package, support and conformance process | Tests willingness to pay and repeatability | Paid continuation, reduced onboarding effort, referenceable outcome |

Failure at a gate should narrow the product claim. Later model performance
cannot compensate for failed ownership, evidence, replay, or parity gates.

## 9. Use of capital

Suggested categories, without assigning amounts before financing scope is
known:

1. **Kernel and runtime engineering:** failure grammar, operation edges,
   concurrent ledger safety, identities, provider ports, allocator, and
   security hardening.
2. **Reference integrations:** NAO parity, Watson/software engineering, and one
   non-NAO qualification.
3. **Evaluation infrastructure:** frozen suites, failure injection,
   model/provider matrix, Workbench ablations, and regression reporting.
4. **Observatory product:** evidence views, comparison workflows, export, and
   operator experience.
5. **Domain onboarding:** tooling, validators, templates, documentation, and
   design-partner delivery.
6. **Commercial readiness:** licensing review, security review, deployment
   packaging, customer discovery, and support process.

Hiring should follow the gates. Early priorities are systems/runtime
engineering, evaluation and reliability, and domain integration. Sales hiring
before repeatable onboarding would be premature.

## 10. Metrics

### Technical qualification

- accepted-task rate on frozen cases;
- unsafe or out-of-scope dispatch rate;
- evidence-complete terminal judgment rate;
- replay determinism;
- stale-evidence and false-completion detection;
- parity disagreement rate and classified causes;
- latency, token, tool-call, memory, and model-lease cost;
- provider/model substitution success under fixed role requirements;
- Workbench holdout uplift and counterexample recovery.

### Product and onboarding

- time to first shadow trace;
- time to approved DomainContractPack;
- percentage of reused kernel and onboarding artifacts;
- custom adapter code per environment;
- qualification-case coverage;
- incident diagnosis time;
- operator acceptance and evidence-review completion.

### Commercial

- design-partner conversion to paid continuation;
- deployment and support revenue mix;
- onboarding gross effort and trend by customer;
- time from discovery to shadow qualification;
- number of qualified roles and environments;
- recurring renewal or expansion;
- referenceability and partner-led introductions.

No baseline values are claimed yet. Each metric needs a definition and data
collection plan before it is used externally.

## 11. Competitive framing

UAH should not compete on having the largest tool catalog or claiming the most
autonomy. Its intended differentiation is the combination of:

- frame-relative semantic task compilation;
- two distinct admission layers;
- environment-owner effect evidence;
- stable agent identity separated from provider capacity;
- multi-actor replay and observability;
- quarantined adaptive memory and candidate search.

Adjacent products may provide workflow orchestration, agent frameworks,
sandboxes, model gateways, observability, or memory. UAH can integrate with
these as runtimes or adapters if they preserve the contracts. A formal
competitive matrix requires current product research and should be created as
a separate sourced deliverable.

## 12. Diligence disclosures

- H0 is a tested contract proof, not a production release.
- H1 agent lifecycle and live provider operation are incomplete.
- H2 NAO planner parity and model qualification are not complete.
- Tool-call budgets are consumed atomically at dispatch, and retry/timeout
  decisions use compiled limits. Provider-consumed model budgets, token/cost
  limits, and hardware allocation remain open.
- The common ledger has atomic commit framing, restart replay, and advisory-lock
  coordination among cooperating processes. It remains local JSONL and rejects
  rather than repairs a crash-truncated commit.
- NeuralWorkbench improvement is unproven and remains an H3 experiment.
- The intended NeuralWorkbench revision is documented, but its gitlink is
  absent from the current UAH tree.
- Observatory has an initial static ledger renderer but no complete product
  view across actors, configurations, providers, comparisons, or Workbench.
- Cross-domain universality is not established.
- There are no customer, revenue, pricing, market-size, compliance, security
  certification, or production-scale claims in the repository.
- Licensing and intellectual-property strategy require review before external
  distribution or commercial packaging.
